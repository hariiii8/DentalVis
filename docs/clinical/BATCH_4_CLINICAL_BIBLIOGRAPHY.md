### 1. Complete Removable Dentures (`complete-removable-dentures`)
- **Canonical ID:** `33333333-3333-4333-a333-333333333331`
- **Primary Department:** `PROS` | **Tier:** 1 | **Status:** `DRAFT`
- **Primary Verified Reference:**
  - **Authors:** Felton D, Cooper L, Duqum I, Minsley G, Guckes A, Haug S, Meredith P, Solie C, Avery D, Chandler N; American College of Prosthodontists.
  - **Title:** *Evidence-based guidelines for the care and maintenance of complete dentures: a publication of the American College of Prosthodontists.*
  - **Journal / Source:** *The Journal of the American Dental Association* (JADA), 2011; 142(Suppl 1): 1S–20S.
  - **DOI:** [`10.14219/jada.archive.2011.0067`](https://doi.org/10.14219/jada.archive.2011.0067)
- **Secondary Verified Reference:**
  - **Authors:** Zarb GA, Hobkirk JA, Eckert SE, Jacob RF.
  - **Title:** *Prosthodontic Treatment for Edentulous Patients: Complete Dentures and Implant-Supported Prostheses*, 13th ed.
  - **Publisher:** Elsevier Mosby, St. Louis, 2013. ISBN: 978-0-323-07844-3.
- **Supported Clinical Claims:**
  - Complete removable dentures rest directly on the mucosa-covered residual alveolar ridge.
  - Retention, stability, and support depend on tissue adaptation, peripheral seal, atmospheric pressure, salivary film capillary action, and neuromuscular control.

  - Recommended hygiene: non-abrasive denture cleansers, daily soaking, never boiling or exposing to hot water (which warps acrylic), and removing dentures overnight to prevent denture stomatitis (*Candida albicans* infection) and reduce residual ridge resorption.

  - **Publisher:** Elsevier, 2016. ISBN: 978-0-323-33990-2.
- **Secondary Verified Reference:**
  - **Authors:** Campbell SD, Cooper L, Craddock H, Hyde TP, Nattress B, Pavitt SH, Seymour DW.
  - **Title:** *Removable partial dentures: The clinical need for innovation.*
  - **Journal / Source:** *The Journal of Prosthetic Dentistry*, 2017; 118(3): 273–280.
  - **DOI:** [`10.1016/j.prosdent.2017.01.008`](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.prosdent.2017.01.008)
- **Supported Clinical Claims:**
  - Removable partial dentures replace missing teeth in partially edentulous arches by utilizing remaining natural abutment teeth and edentulous ridges for support and retention.
  - Key components: rigid major connector, direct retainers (clasps), rests (delivering vertical occlusal forces along the long axis of abutments), and denture base with artificial teeth.
  - Potential risks include plaque accumulation on abutment teeth (elevating caries and periodontal disease risks if meticulous oral hygiene is not practiced) and rotational forces on distal-extension saddles (Kennedy Class I & II).

  - **Authors:** Proffit WR, Fields HW, Larson B, Sarver DM.
  - **Title:** *Contemporary Orthodontics*, 6th Edition.
  - **Publisher:** Elsevier, 2019. ISBN: 978-0-323-54387-3.
- **Secondary Verified Reference:**
  - **Authors:** Papageorgiou SN, Papadelli AA, Eliades T.
  - **Title:** *Effect of orthodontic treatment with fixed appliances on periodontal status: a systematic review and meta-analysis.*
  - **Journal / Source:** *Journal of Dentistry*, 2018; 77: 1–12.
  - **DOI:** [`10.1016/j.jdent.2018.07.009`](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jdent.2018.07.009)
- **Supported Clinical Claims:**
  - Fixed braces use bonded brackets (metal or ceramic) and sequential continuous archwires to deliver light, continuous mechanical forces that induce bone remodeling via compression (osteoclastic resorption) and tension (osteoblastic apposition) in the periodontal ligament.
  - Treatment addresses crowding, spacing, rotations, deep bite, open bite, and crossbite.
  - Known clinical risks include enamel demineralization (white spot lesions) around brackets if plaque hygiene is suboptimal, mild apical external root resorption (averaging 1–2 mm), and transient post-activation discomfort lasting 24–72 hours.

### 6. Palatal Expander (`palatal-expander`)
- **Canonical ID:** `33333333-3333-4333-a333-333333333335`
- **Primary Department:** `ORTH` | **Secondary Department:** `PEDO` | **Tier:** 2 | **Status:** `DRAFT`
- **Primary Verified Reference:**
  - **Authors:** Baccetti T, Franchi L, Cameron CG, McNamara JA Jr.
  - **Title:** *Treatment timing for rapid maxillary expansion.*
  - **Journal / Source:** *The Angle Orthodontist*, 2001; 71(5): 343–350.
  - **DOI:** [`10.1043/0003-3219(2001)071<0343:TTFRME>2.0.CO;2`](https://doi.org/10.1043/0003-3219(2001)071<0343:TTFRME>2.0.CO;2)
- **Secondary Verified Reference:**
  - **Authors:** Suri L, Taneja P.
  - **Title:** *Surgically assisted rapid palatal expansion: thinking above the box.*
  - **Journal / Source:** *American Journal of Orthodontics and Dentofacial Orthopedics*, 2008; 133(2): 290–302.
  - **DOI:** [`10.1016/j.ajodo.2007.09.006`](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ajodo.2007.09.006)
- **Supported Clinical Claims:**
  - Rapid maxillary expansion (RME) widens a narrow maxillary arch and corrects posterior crossbites by separating the midpalatal suture before its complete interdigitation/fusion.
  - Optimal timing is during late mixed or early permanent dentition (pre-pubertal / early pubertal growth spurt); skeletal response diminishes after suture maturation (often requiring surgically assisted RPE or MARPE in skeletally mature post-adolescents).
  - Typical activation protocol: turns of the jackscrew once or twice daily for 2–3 weeks, followed by 3–6 months of passive retention to permit bone mineralization in the widened suture. Midline diastema routinely appears during active expansion and spontaneously closes as transeptal fibers pull central incisors together.

- **Secondary Verified Reference:**
  - **Authors:** Innes NP, Ricketts D, Chong LY, Keightley AJ, Lamont T, Santamaria RM.
  - **Title:** *Preformed crowns for decayed primary molar teeth.*
  - **Journal / Source:** *Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews*, 2015; 2015(12): CD005512.

- **Supported Clinical Claims:**

  - May be placed via conventional tooth preparation (occlusal reduction and interproximal slicing) or the Hall Technique (cementation without local anesthesia or caries removal in specific non-symptomatic indicated cases).

  - **Authors:** Lin YT, Lin WH, Lin YT.
  - **Title:** *A long-term evaluation of fixed space maintainers in children.*
  - **Journal / Source:** *Journal of Dental Sciences*, 2011; 6(4): 205–209.
  - **DOI:** [`10.1016/j.jds.2011.09.006`](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jds.2011.09.006)

  - Regular clinical and radiographic follow-up is mandatory until the succedaneous tooth is actively erupting, at which point the appliance is removed.
